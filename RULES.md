# Abba Letters — The Game (working title)

A Codenames-style team game played with the 365 Abba Letters paintings
instead of word cards. Clues may be evocative phrases (a nod to Dixit),
but the rules and scoring are pure Codenames: simple, fast, decisive.

## What you need

- 4+ players, split into two teams: **Gold** and **Blue**
- Each team picks one **Storyteller** (the clue-giver). Everyone else is a **Guesser**.
- A 5×5 grid of 25 paintings, dealt fresh from the 365 every round.

## Setup

The Storytellers see a secret key for the grid:

- **9 paintings** belong to the starting team (Gold starts round 1, then alternates)
- **8 paintings** belong to the other team
- **7 paintings** are **Wanderers** (neutral)
- **1 painting** is the **Deceiver** (the assassin — never touch it)

Guessers see only the paintings. Storytellers see the color key.

## How a turn works

1. The Storyteller studies the grid and gives a **clue**: a short
   evocative phrase (up to 8 words) plus a **number**.
   - Example: *"bread for the journey — 3"*
   - Unlike classic Codenames, the clue may be poetic or descriptive —
     that's the Dixit spirit. It still must point at YOUR team's
     paintings, and one clue per turn.
2. The number says how many of your paintings the clue points to.
   Your team may guess up to **number + 1** paintings (the bonus guess).
3. Guessers discuss openly, then tap paintings one at a time:
   - **Your painting** → covered in your color. Guess again (while guesses remain).
   - **Wanderer** → covered gray. Turn ends.
   - **Opponents' painting** → covered in their color (you helped them!). Turn ends.
   - **The Deceiver** → your team **loses immediately**.
4. Your team may stop guessing voluntarily after at least one guess.

## Winning

- First team to cover **all** of its paintings wins the round.
- Touch the Deceiver and you lose on the spot — no matter the score.

## Clue rules (house rules, enforced by honor)

- The clue phrase must be about what is *in* or *evoked by* the paintings —
  no naming the day number, no "the third one on the left."
- Storytellers: subtle beats obvious. Point at two or three paintings
  with one beautiful phrase and your team will love you for it.
